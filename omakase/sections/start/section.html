How it started

In October 2025 I was stuck in bed for a few days. I'd seen the discussion, first about Omakub, then Omarchy for a while. With nothing much better to do, it seemed like a great time to try something new. However, I didn't want to mess up my work MacBook (and soon learned Omarchy won't install on it anyway). All I happened to have was a 2012 MacBook Pro, long out of service, with a sketchy battery and a keyboard that was falling apart. But that's all it took.

Opus 4.1 had been out since summer. We weren't quite at the inflection point of Opus 4.5, but the early days of the Opus 4.x series still held a lot of promise. I was also using a machine I didn't really care about and otherwise would slowly be degrading in the drawer. Case in point: the 8 key didn't work. And seemingly every password or 2FA I needed to type had an 8.

Without much expectation, I told Claude to fix my keyboard. I knew a mechanical fix was not possible, but Claude dutifully ran through a bunch of options, trying different layers of fixes and tools. Finally it landed on a keyd mapping of F12. Not exactly the most intuitive, but it worked, and I could log in again.

I'd used Linux as my primary computer in college (my Windows machine was required in the living room to play pirated TV and movies). This was a few years before my first iBook. CS 1312 had gotten us on Emacs, and it was a great workspace for a CS major. But when I got an iBook a few years later, I had most of the power of Linux and everything looked a lot better, so my Linux usage was relegated to running mod_perl (and later FastCGI and Mongrel).

Back in 2025, I was feeling my way through Hyprland and Neovim. I got to work on my dotfiles and tried on a bunch of themes. I was already in the habit of asking Claude to change something about the machine. Soon I had everything dialed in, and once I was out of bed, I knew I needed to try the key kung-fu with my beloved mechanical keyboard. Thanks to a Dell desktop we were experimenting with, I was soon up and running on a respectable modern computer.

When Quattro launched and there was a huge run on plugins, I was reminded of the early days of Rails, when everyone had acts_as_this or whatever_fu. This time it was oma*. It seemed everyone wanted to shove everything imaginable into a little menu bar widget and, once it passed the review process (shouts to you, HANCORE), feel like they were participating in this new project. And that is great!

But a little plugin is not what makes Omarchy great. And linking to a catalogue listing leaves us missing context and hype. What I want to see is how you've truly made your computer your own. My ideas and customizations are almost certainly not for you. And that's great! But there may be the seed of an idea in here that you can run with. And with agents, all you need to do is pull down the repo, tell it to borrow the good ideas and mold the rest to your liking.
